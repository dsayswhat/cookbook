# Charcoal Grilled Burgers and Dogs

**Watch the technique:** [Grilling burgers and hot dogs over charcoal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SI-p2kxb69w)

## Ingredients

### Hand-Formed Burgers
- 2 lbs ground beef
- Garlic salt, generously
- Black pepper, coarsely ground, generously
- Worcestershire sauce, a generous few dashes

### Store-Bought Frozen Patties
- Frozen hamburger patties, as many as you need
- Garlic salt
- Worcestershire sauce

### Hot Dogs
- Hot dogs (they are already fully cooked)

### For the Fire
- Charcoal, enough to fill a chimney starter three-quarters full
- 4 segments of paper towel
- Cooking spray or oil
- Buns, cheese, and condiments to serve

## Instructions

1. Make the patties first. Put the ground beef in a bowl with plenty of garlic salt, plenty of black pepper, and plenty of Worcestershire sauce. Mash it all up until the seasoning is worked through — you should be able to see the black specks of pepper in the meat.
2. Cut the 2 lbs of beef into quarters, then cut each quarter in half again, for 8 equal chunks. Roll each into a ball, then smash it flat between two paper plates. Stack the patties on a baking sheet with parchment paper between them so they don't stick to each other.
3. Light the fire. Take about 4 segments of paper towel, spray them with cooking oil, fold them up, and stuff them underneath the bottom grate of the chimney starter. Fill the chimney three-quarters full with charcoal — no more, or it gets too hot. Light the paper towel with a lighter.
4. Let the chimney burn about 15 minutes, until the coals on top are starting to catch fire. Pour them out into the grill and spread them around. Consider piling them a little heavier toward one side so you keep a cooler zone — not all on one side, just enough that you have somewhere to retreat to. Use an oven mitt; the chimney is hot.
5. Let the grate warm up for 4 to 5 minutes, then lay the burgers on.
6. Watch for the juice. The patties will start to bead and juice on top, a little like pancakes getting bubbles. When a burger is juicing all over the top, it's ready to flip. If it's only juicing in one spot, give it more time.
7. Flip along the grate, not against it. Come at the burger from the side, running the spatula parallel with the bars — the same way you'd slide under French toast. Approaching at a right angle fights the grate and tears the patty.
8. For frozen store-bought patties, season them right on the grill: a good shake of garlic salt on each, then a splash of Worcestershire over the top. It doesn't matter that some runs off. Once you flip them, season the second side the same way. Frozen patties cook slower than the hand-formed ones, so give them extra time and expect less browning.
9. Put the hot dogs on the cooler side, laid lengthwise along the bars rather than across them — that way they stay put and you can control them. They're already fully cooked, so you're only waiting for them to get sweaty and take on color. Give them a quarter turn, then another quarter turn, until they're evenly darkened.
10. Check the burgers with an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part. The juices should run clear rather than red. 145°F is not done — pull them at 155°F to 165°F.
11. Rest the burgers a minute, then build them on buns with whatever you like.

## Notes

Three-quarters of a chimney is the whole trick. Fill it fuller and the grill runs too hot: the fat renders fast, the juices hit the coals, and you get flare-ups licking up around the burgers. At three-quarters you get browning instead of burning.

The seasoning amounts are the one thing not specified — season by eye until you can see pepper throughout the meat, and taste-adjust on the next batch. Two pounds makes 8 patties at a quarter pound each.

The 155–165°F range straddles the USDA's 160°F recommendation for ground beef. If you're cooking for kids, older relatives, or anyone pregnant, stay at 160°F or above.

Keep a cool zone on the grill at all times. When a burger starts to burn or a flare-up catches it, move it over there and it will save itself.
